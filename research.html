Research

Many real-world risks remain difficult to manage because existing approaches are often reactive, rely on limited or opaque evidence, and provide insufficient support for timely intervention.

My research develops trustworthy multimodal AI that models and proactively detects emerging risks, provides evidence-grounded explanations, and supports actionable interventions across urban systems, mental health, and industrial safety.

Proactive Bulk Refund Booking Risk Management

Research Partner · SR

◆ Urban Systems Behavioral Logs

In a large-scale Korean railway ticketing system, Bulk Refund Booking (BRB) occurs when users strategically reserve multiple tickets and later refund unused bookings for personal gain. This behavior distorts demand signals: seats may physically remain available but appear sold out to other passengers, while late refunds can leave operators unable to resell the released inventory.

I analyze large-scale railway booking and refund histories to detect emerging BRB risks early, estimate when those risks may materialize, and generate behavior-grounded explanations that support timely review, policy action, and seat-inventory recovery.

Research Progression. This research has progressed from proactive user-level BRB detection to a time-to-event formulation that estimates both the likelihood of future risk and the remaining time available for operational intervention.

Personalized Depression Detection from Multimodal Passive Sensing

◆ Mental Health Passive Sensing

Depression-related behavioral changes are highly individualized and context-dependent, making population-level risk models difficult to generalize across individuals.

This research models personalized depression risk by retrieving behaviorally similar historical contexts from multimodal passive sensing data and using those contexts to generate individualized, evidence-grounded risk assessments.

Vision-Language Models for Industrial Anomaly Detection

Research Partner · KEPRI

◆ Industrial Safety Images Language

Industrial defects are often subtle, heterogeneous, and difficult to describe using fixed anomaly categories, while conventional anomaly detectors frequently provide scores without sufficient visual evidence.

This research studies how large vision-language models can understand normal industrial structures, identify fine-grained defects, and generate reliable explanations grounded in visual evidence for inspection and maintenance decisions.
